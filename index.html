Research

A paper I'm writing.

paper in progress

Controlling Parameter Drift in LoRA Continual Test-Time Adaptation via Reset Detection

When a model adapts to new data at test time, it can drift — over-fitting to a shifting domain until it forgets what it knew. I'm studying whether freezing the base weights and adapting only the LoRA layers keeps that in check, and whether watching the model's prediction entropy can detect the moment drift sets in.

When entropy spikes past a threshold, the system dynamically resets the adaptation. The question: does reset detection help more as the domain shifts get more severe? That's what the experiments are built to measure.

About

I'm Neel Bansal. Most of my projects start the same way — I notice a thing people are doing badly by hand (texting one person to log 150 students' service hours; losing instruments to damage nobody reported; scattering sheet music across a dozen Google Classrooms) and build the system that should have existed.

Some of it is competition work: a scholarship-matching platform that took 2nd at a Microsoft hackathon, and a public-safety awareness map that won 1st at Boston University. Some of it is just curiosity — a relativity simulator, a programming language made of slang, a paper on how models forget. And two of them — Freshline and Instrumentify — I'm turning into companies.
